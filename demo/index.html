Reading on a screen is harder than it looks

By A. Writer · 8 min read

When text is surrounded by banners, sidebars, autoplaying video and a dozen other things competing for attention, the eye has to work harder to stay on the line it is reading. For most people this is a minor annoyance. For readers with ADHD or dyslexia it can be the difference between finishing a page and giving up.

The fix does not have to be clever. In special education, a simple reading ruler — a strip of card with a slot cut out — has been used for decades. It does one thing: it hides everything except the line you are on.

The problem

Digital pages rarely offer that kind of focus. A typical article page loads a header, a newsletter modal, a cookie banner, a chat widget and a floating video player. Each one is designed to pull the eye away at the exact moment the reader is trying to concentrate.

Reading researchers call this visual crowding. The words are perfectly legible on their own; the difficulty comes from everything else on the page. Remove the competition and the same paragraph becomes noticeably easier to finish.

A reading line does not make the text bigger or brighter. It makes everything else quieter.

How it works

The reading line is a single band, positioned where the eyes rest. Above and below it the page is dimmed, so the next line is still visible but no longer competes for attention. The band can be dragged, resized, or locked to the line of text under the pointer.

Because the line is only an overlay, it works on almost any page: long-form articles, documentation, dashboards, online textbooks, and even web apps built on a canvas. The page itself is not modified, so links, forms and selection keep working.

Results

In a small internal study, readers who used a line reported fewer regressions — going back to re-read the same sentence — and finished long passages slightly faster. The effect was largest for readers who already used a ruler on paper.

The same idea transfers to documents. Papers, reports and textbooks are often read in the browser, and a line that only works on web pages misses half of a student's reading. That is why the reading line also works inside a built-in PDF reader, with the same controls and the same keyboard shortcuts.

Conclusion

Focus is not a personality trait; it is a property of the page. Give a reader one line of calm and the rest of the text gets out of the way.
